## 🎯 Business Problem

A retail superstore operating across 4 regions in the United States needed to understand:
- Which regions, categories, and products are driving revenue
- Where sales are declining or underperforming
- How to allocate marketing and operational resources more effectively
- What customer and shipping patterns reveal about business opportunities

---

## 🔍 Key Insights

### 📦 Category Performance
> **Technology dominates at 37% ($827K) of total sales** — nearly double Office Supplies and well ahead of Furniture. Technology is clearly the revenue engine of this business.

### 🗺️ Regional Performance
> **West region leads at 31% ($710K)** while **Central is massively untapped at only 22% ($493K)**. Despite similar market conditions, Central lags behind — representing a significant growth opportunity if the right products and marketing are applied.

### 📈 Sales Growth Trend
> **Sales grew 50%+ from 2015 ($480K) to 2018 ($722K)** — a consistent upward trajectory despite a brief dip in 2016. The business is scaling and the trend is firmly upward with no signs of slowing.

### 🚚 Shipping Behaviour
> **60% of customers choose Standard Class shipping** — the dominant preference. But **Same Day delivery sits at just 5% (538 orders)** — this is a hidden upsell opportunity. A targeted promotion or pricing strategy for Same Day could unlock a significant new revenue stream.

### 📅 Quarterly Seasonality
> **Q4 is the crown quarter — every single year, no exceptions 👑** Q4 consistently delivers the highest sales ($871K across all years), driven by seasonal demand. Q1 is always the weakest ($351K). Businesses should front-load inventory and marketing investment ahead of Q4.

### 🏆 Top Products
> **Canon imageCLASS 2200 Advanced Copier is the #1 product at $61.6K** — nearly double the second-best product ($27.5K). This single SKU is a critical revenue driver and should be prioritized for stock availability and promotional focus.

### 👤 Top Customers
> **Sean Miller leads with $25K across 15 orders** — the highest spending customer. However, **Raymond Buch places 18 orders but only generates $15K**, suggesting he buys lower-value items. These are two distinct customer behaviors worth targeting differently.

### 🏙️ Top Cities
> **New York City dominates with 891 orders and $252K in sales** — more than 3x Los Angeles ($173K). Tier 1 cities drive disproportionate volume, but there is headroom in mid-tier cities like Houston and Chicago.

## 💡 Business Recommendations

1. **Invest in the West, fix the Central** — West proves the model works. Replicate those strategies (product mix, pricing, promotions) in Central to close the 9% gap.

2. **Protect the Canon copier** — The #1 product generates nearly double its nearest competitor. Ensure it's always in stock, featured prominently, and offered as a bundle for Corporate customers.

3. **Launch a Same Day shipping promotion** — 60% of customers default to Standard Class, likely out of habit or price sensitivity. A limited-time Same Day discount could shift behavior and unlock premium revenue.

4. **Pre-load Q4 inventory and marketing** — Q4 is the strongest quarter every year without exception. Businesses should increase ad spend, inventory buffers, and sales team capacity entering October.

5. **Segment and target Raymond Buch-type customers** — High order frequency but low spend suggests a customer who buys cheap items repeatedly. A targeted upsell campaign (bundle deals, premium alternatives) could significantly increase their lifetime value.
